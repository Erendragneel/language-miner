# Picture clue rules

An alphabet symbol does not necessarily name an object. Character cards therefore connect an actual pictured example word to the target symbol. Most picture questions show a spelling gap. These do not claim that silent letters, internal letters, or spelling variants all represent a word's first sound.

Japanese vowel cards retain the established ant, dog, rabbit, pencil, and onigiri first-sound examples. Other Japanese examples use kana readings. Katakana readings are practice transcriptions, not a claim that all example words are normally written in katakana.

Korean examples split one displayed syllable into its leading consonant, vowel, and optional final consonant. The blank targets exactly one component. The implementation uses Unicode's modern Hangul syllable decomposition. Source: [Unicode Korean FAQ](https://www.unicode.org/faq/korean.html).

Thai spelling clues use real example words containing the target character or vowel sign. A traditional letter-name association, where supplied, must be identified as such; obsolete letters must not be presented as letters in modern spellings. Reference: [Northern Illinois University's Thai consonant table](https://seasite.niu.edu/Thai/basic/alpha_cons_table.htm).

Source-word meanings and their editorial limitations remain documented in VOCABULARY-REVIEW.md. Exact spelling checks do not certify a full native-language editorial review. No unrelated cavern background or shape-only replacement is used when artwork is missing.

Mandarin clues use a pictured Chinese word and its pinyin. Initial-letter questions, final-vowel questions, and tone-mark questions are distinct. The pictured word's recording is used in listening mode. Final-vowel blanks intentionally omit the tone mark while the completed example shows it. Reference: [Yoyo Chinese pinyin chart](https://yoyochinese.com/chinese-learning-tools/Mandarin-Chinese-pronunciation-lesson/pinyin-chart-table).

Flag diagrams use the correct basic proportions and designs: [Japan's national flag](https://www.japan.go.jp/japan/flagandanthem/index.html), [Thailand's flag specifications](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/flagspec_Flag-of-the-Kingdom-of-Thailand.pdf), and [Qatar's flag ratio](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/450708-longest-national-flag-official-aspect-ratio). They illustrate country names or the word flag, not an unrelated sound.

Calendar questions use a fixed fictional reference date, 16 November 2026. This is the date inside the exercise, not the computer's current date. Gold indicates the target date; a blue outline marks the reference day. Quantity, color, flag, QR, shape, and calendar visuals are semantic diagrams made in SVG.

Map clues use Natural Earth 1:110m country geometry, rendered as original SVG diagrams for the Africa, world, and geography entries. The source data is public domain: https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/ . Dataset: https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/master/geojson/ne_110m_admin_0_countries.geojson . The maps are learning illustrations, not navigation maps.
